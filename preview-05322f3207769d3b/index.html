Hi, I’m Xiaosha — also known as Evelyne. I’m a researcher, engineer, and composer pursuing dual master’s degrees in Computational Science and Engineering and Music Technology at the Georgia Institute of Technology.

My research focuses on cross-modal representation learning, particularly how learned representations connect perception and reasoning, and how they could develop together. I currently study source-aware understanding across simultaneous audio contexts and the geometry of musical representations, using interventions to investigate whether attributes such as pitch and timbre can be disentangled.

I am honored to be advised by Pan Li, Alexander Lerch, and Ziyu Wang (Courant Institute, NYU). I work closely with Chun Liu, Ju-Chiang Wang, Xinjie Shen, and Yongyi Zang (Smule Labs).

As a Research Scientist Intern at Smule Labs, I work on measuring modality dependence in multimodal benchmarks and building scalable synthetic-data and evaluation systems.

Ongoing work

Source-Aware Audio Understanding in Parallel Multi-Context Settings

How can models track source-specific content across simultaneous speech contexts and use dependencies between them for many-to-many reasoning?

Representation Geometry and Post-Hoc Disentanglement in Audio Models

Investigating whether frozen MusicGen and EnCodec representations contain low-dimensional linear subspaces shared across token IDs that allow pitch to vary while preserving timbre. Through representation interventions and audio decoding, I test how this shared structure generalizes to held-out IDs, pitches, timbres, and contexts, evaluating pitch change and timbre preservation separately.

How Much of the Modality Does a Question Need?

Manuscript in preparation for TMLR.

Xiaosha Li, Y. Zang, Q. Yang, Pan Li

Per-Degree Benchmark: a per-item, reliability-certified measurement of modality dependence, using progressively richer textual evidence to distinguish perceptual requirements from textual shortcuts.

Predictive Rhythm Perception in Music Generative Models

Identifying context-driven rhythmic expectations in music generative models, including prediction signals at omitted events and delayed adaptation to abrupt tempo changes.

Long-Horizon Multimodal Investigation

Studying how systems gather distributed evidence, revise hypotheses, and reason across modalities over extended interactions.

Learning Adaptive Temporal Representations for Music

Investigating whether learned, content-dependent segmentation can reveal useful basic units of music, compressing redundant structure while preserving timing and expressive detail beyond uniform frame-based representations.

Musical Sketches as Generative Controls

Exploring how humming, melody sketches, and embedding interventions can guide music generation while preserving a musician’s intended pitch and timing.

Modeling Jazz Improvisation

Investigating how generative models represent and generate jazz improvisation, and how musical context shapes their improvisational choices.

Related projects & technical reports

Spectral Probing of Frozen Audio Encoders

Decomposing audio embeddings along the time axis with FFT and probing individual temporal-frequency bands on downstream tasks to investigate how task-relevant information is encoded in slowly and rapidly varying components of the representations.

Art & background

I graduated summa cum laude from Berklee College of Music with a Bachelor of Music in Electronic Production and Design and Composition. I studied with Dr. Richard Boulanger and took masterclasses with Du Yun (Pulitzer Prize winner), Richard Carrick (Guggenheim Fellow; Chair of Composition at Berklee), Julia Wolfe (Pulitzer Prize winner), and Nan Schwartz (Grammy Award winner).

I have been a singer for 22 years and a dancer for 18 years. My performances have taken me to the United Nations Headquarters in New York and on cultural-exchange tours across Australia, Indonesia, and Cambodia. My compositions span chamber music, electroacoustic work, art song, and music for dance, with performances by SPLICE Ensemble and Sound Icon Sinfonietta. I received an Honorable Mention in the MTNA composition competition (Massachusetts, 2021) and the Amy Beach Award, a Berklee Composition Department scholarship (2022).

Before Berklee, I earned a BS in Business and Management, magna cum laude, at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute, completing the four-year program in two years. I also studied at Hunan University’s College of Finance and Statistics, admitted through its high-level vocal arts pathway with a first-place examination result and an admission scholarship. In 2016, I attended summer programs at Magdalene College, University of Cambridge, and Oriel College, University of Oxford.
